Most "free" video editing software either has annoying watermarks, hidden paywalls, or runs terribly on average PCs. Here are the ones that actually work.

## What Makes Software Truly Free?

Before we start, let's define "free":
- No watermarks on exported videos
- No feature restrictions that force you to upgrade
- No trial period that expires
- Actually usable without paying

Many "free" tools fail these tests. The ones below pass.

## 1. CapCut (Best Overall for Beginners)

**Why it's good:**
- Actually easy to use
- Works on low-end PCs
- Great for social media
- Free with no catches

**Why it might not be for you:**
- Limited advanced features
- Cloud-based (needs internet for some features)
- Not ideal for long videos

**Best for:** TikTok, Instagram, YouTube Shorts

**Learning curve:** Easy - You'll be editing in 10 minutes

**PC requirements:** Works on almost anything

## 2. DaVinci Resolve (Best Free Professional Tool)

**Why it's good:**
- Completely free, no watermarks
- Professional-grade features
- Amazing color correction
- Used by actual filmmakers

**Why it might not be for you:**
- Steep learning curve
- Heavy on PC resources
- Overwhelming for simple projects

**Best for:** Serious YouTube videos, short films

**Learning curve:** Hard - Takes weeks to feel comfortable

**PC requirements:** 8GB RAM minimum, 16GB recommended, needs good GPU

## 3. Shotcut (Best for Mid-Range Editing)

**Why it's good:**
- Simple interface
- Lightweight and fast
- No watermarks ever
- Works offline

**Why it might not be for you:**
- Looks outdated
- Fewer effects than competitors
- Limited audio tools

**Best for:** Basic YouTube videos, family videos

**Learning curve:** Medium - Takes a few days

**PC requirements:** Works on most PCs with 4GB+ RAM

## 4. Clipchamp (Best for Quick Edits)

**Why it's good:**
- Browser-based, no installation
- Built into Windows 11
- Very simple interface
- Templates for quick projects

**Why it might not be for you:**
- Limited export quality on free plan
- Requires internet connection
- Basic features compared to others

**Best for:** Quick projects, presentations, simple edits

**Learning curve:** Easy - 5 minutes to start

**PC requirements:** Just needs a web browser

## What About Mobile?

If you mostly edit on your phone:
- **CapCut Mobile** - Best overall
- **InShot** - Good alternative
- **iMovie (iOS only)** - Simple and clean

## Which One Should You Choose?

### Choose CapCut if:
- You're editing for social media
- You want to start editing TODAY
- Your PC is old or low-end
- You edit on mobile too

### Choose DaVinci Resolve if:
- You want professional skills
- Your PC is decent
- You're serious about video editing
- You have time to learn

### Choose Shotcut if:
- You want balance between simple and powerful
- You need offline editing
- You don't like CapCut or DaVinci

### Choose Clipchamp if:
- You need to edit NOW without installing anything
- Your projects are simple
- You use Windows 11

## Avoid These "Free" Tools

**Windows Movie Maker:** Discontinued, use Clipchamp instead

**Lightworks Free:** Too limited, confusing interface, pushy upsells

**VSDC Free:** Buggy, unstable, exports fail randomly

**Any tool with watermarks in the free version:** Not truly free

## The Beginner's Path

**Start here:** CapCut (1-2 months)
Learn the basics, finish actual projects

**Then move to:** DaVinci Resolve or Adobe Premiere
When you need more power and are ready to learn

Don't start with professional tools if you're a complete beginner. You'll get frustrated and quit.

## Common Beginner Mistakes

**Mistake 1:** "I'll learn the most powerful tool first"
Reality: You'll get overwhelmed and quit

**Mistake 2:** "I'll download every free tool and compare"
Reality: You'll waste weeks without finishing a single video

**Mistake 3:** "Free tools must be bad"
Reality: CapCut and DaVinci Resolve are genuinely excellent

## Final Recommendation

**If you're reading this and still can't decide, download CapCut.**

It's free, simple, and you'll actually use it. If you outgrow it in 6 months, that's a good problem to have. Move to DaVinci Resolve then.

Starting with the easiest tool and actually finishing videos beats starting with the best tool and quitting in frustration.

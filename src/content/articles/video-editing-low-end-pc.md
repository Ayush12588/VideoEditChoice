Got an old laptop or budget PC? Most video editing software will make it sound like a jet engine and lag constantly. Here's what actually works.

## What Counts as a Low-End PC?

For this guide, low-end means:
- 4GB RAM or less
- No dedicated GPU (integrated graphics only)
- Old processor (5+ years old)
- Limited storage space
- Laptop that gets hot quickly

If this describes your computer, keep reading.

## Software That Actually Works

### 1. CapCut Desktop

**Why it works on low-end PCs:**
- Lightweight and optimized
- Doesn't need a powerful GPU
- Handles 1080p without much lag
- Small file size

**Performance:**
- 4GB RAM: Works, but close other apps
- Integrated graphics: Runs fine
- Export time: Slower but manageable

**Best for:** TikTok, Instagram, YouTube Shorts, simple YouTube videos

**The catch:** Limited features compared to professional tools

### 2. Shotcut

**Why it works on low-end PCs:**
- Lightweight compared to DaVinci or Premiere
- Works on older systems
- No GPU required
- Offline editing (no cloud overhead)

**Performance:**
- 4GB RAM: Usable for 1080p
- Integrated graphics: Works
- Export time: Moderate

**Best for:** Basic YouTube videos, family videos, school projects

**The catch:** Interface looks dated, fewer effects

### 3. OpenShot

**Why it works on low-end PCs:**
- Simple and lightweight
- Very basic features = less resource usage
- Free and open source

**Performance:**
- 4GB RAM: Works for basic editing
- Integrated graphics: Fine
- Export time: Reasonable

**Best for:** Very simple projects, beginners who need basic cuts

**The catch:** Limited features, can be buggy

### 4. Clipchamp (Browser-Based)

**Why it works on low-end PCs:**
- Runs in browser, uses cloud processing
- Doesn't stress your PC as much
- No installation needed

**Performance:**
- Requires stable internet
- Processing happens online
- Less strain on your PC

**Best for:** Quick edits, presentations, basic projects

**The catch:** Needs internet, limited free export quality

## Software to AVOID on Low-End PCs

### DaVinci Resolve
**Why:** Needs 8GB+ RAM, strong GPU, will lag terribly

### Adobe Premiere Pro
**Why:** Heavy, resource-hungry, needs subscription and powerful PC

### Final Cut Pro
**Why:** Mac only, needs modern Mac hardware

### Filmora
**Why:** Looks simple but surprisingly heavy on resources

## Tips for Editing on Low-End PCs

### Before You Start

**1. Close everything else**
- Close browser tabs
- Stop background apps
- Disable antivirus temporarily

**2. Use proxy files**
- Edit with lower-quality previews
- Export with full quality
- Most software can do this

**3. Work with 720p or 1080p**
- Don't try 4K editing
- Convert high-res footage before editing

**4. Save frequently**
- Low-end PCs crash more often
- Auto-save every 5 minutes

**5. Clear space**
- Keep at least 20GB free
- Video files are huge

### During Editing

**Keep it simple:**
- Fewer effects = less lag
- Use cuts instead of transitions
- Avoid stacking too many layers

**Render previews:**
- Pre-render complex sections
- Makes playback smoother

**Work in sections:**
- Edit one part at a time
- Don't load entire hour-long projects

## Realistic Expectations

### What Your Low-End PC CAN Handle

- 1080p editing (with patience)
- Basic cuts and transitions
- Simple color correction
- Text overlays
- Audio adjustments

### What It CANNOT Handle

- 4K footage smoothly
- Heavy effects and filters
- Multiple video layers
- Real-time color grading
- Fast rendering

## Mobile Alternative

If your PC struggles too much, consider editing on your phone:

**CapCut Mobile** - Often faster than editing on an old PC
**InShot** - Simple and quick
**iMovie** - If you have an iPhone

Modern phones often edit video better than old PCs.

## Upgrading vs New Software

### Signs You Need a PC Upgrade, Not New Software

- Every software lags constantly
- Exports take many hours
- PC freezes while editing
- Can't edit more than 5 minutes of footage

### Signs Software is the Problem

- Specific software lags, others work fine
- PC handles other tasks well
- Only heavy effects cause problems

If every software is unusable, your PC might be too old for video editing. Consider:
- Mobile editing instead
- Cloud-based editing (Clipchamp)
- Saving for a budget upgrade

## Upgrade Priorities (If You Have Some Budget)

**Best bang for buck:**
1. **RAM upgrade** (4GB → 8GB) - Biggest improvement
2. **SSD** (if you have HDD) - Faster loading
3. **Better CPU** - Only if upgrading whole PC

**Don't bother:**
- GPU upgrade on old system
- Maxing out old PC with expensive parts

## Final Recommendation

**For most low-end PCs: Use CapCut**

It's the best balance of features and performance. It won't turn your old PC into a beast, but it'll work without driving you crazy.

**If CapCut still lags:** Try Clipchamp in your browser. Let the cloud do the heavy lifting.

**If nothing works smoothly:** Your PC might be too old. Consider editing simple clips on your phone instead. CapCut Mobile is surprisingly powerful.

Remember: The best video editing software for your low-end PC is the one you'll actually use. Don't waste time searching for a magical solution that makes your 2015 laptop edit 4K footage smoothly. It doesn't exist.

Focus on creating good content with the tools that work, not finding perfect tools.
